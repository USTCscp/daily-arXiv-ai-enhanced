A subset of the LoRD sample selected to have V-shaped continua ($N=244$) show prominent higher-order Balmer absorption features and [NeV]$λ$3426 emission, both of which would likely be missed in JWST/PRISM observations given the low spectral resolution. A composite SED of the LoRDs differs from a typical quasar SED in the rest-frame UV/optical, but the two agree with each other in the NIR. The LoRD SED matches well with a stack of LRDs and can be modeled either as a reddened AGN combined with a host galaxy, or as a reddened AGN combined with a host galaxy and a cool blackbody. Interestingly, the LoRDs are X-ray detected at a rate comparable to typical quasars. However, the probability that LoRDs and typical quasars would go undetected, if subject to the LRD X-ray upper limits, is $>50\%$.
